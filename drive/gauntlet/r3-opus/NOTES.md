# Drive (r3-opus)

A messenger drives parcels between real Spanish cities, on a road graph baked from Natural Earth, in real time, under the real sun and weather of wherever the car is, with a lo-fi radio playing.

## How to play
Leave it running. The car drives itself, changes lanes around slower traffic, and the camera moves between the cockpit, a chase view and a side view on its own. Every few minutes the driver pulls over by themselves for a coffee or fuel and leans against the car (sometimes a cat turns up).

A tap anywhere starts the sound and shows the HUD for a few seconds: the route, the parcel, the road, the speed, the kilometres left and the arrival time. Its buttons are the two indicators (the car changes lane when it is free), the pedal (»: 90 km/h, »»: 120 km/h, »»»: 120 km/h with time running ten times faster), STOP / GO, RADIO and the sound. A tap on the radio in the dashboard also changes station. Keys: arrows for indicators and pedal, space to stop, R for radio, M for sound.

The radio has two stations. The local one follows the region (flamenco-flavoured in the south, Galician in the north-west, plain lo-fi on the plateau), and the mixtape is a synthwave / city pop style added to the Clockscapes generator.

For testing, `?hour=18.5`, `?weather=rain|snow|fog|cloud|clear`, `?cam=cockpit|chase|side` and `?stop=1` force those states.

## What was reused and made
The Clockscapes `LOFI` music generator is copied in whole, with one new `synthwave` row in `STYLES`, and `sunPos` is copied from Clockscapes' world code. All the art was generated by scripts written for this entry (`tools/make-cockpit.mjs`, `tools/make-cars.mjs`, `tools/make-scenery.mjs`, made by Opus art helpers) into `art/`. The road graph comes from `tools/bake-roads.py`, which reads Natural Earth's `ne_10m_roads` (public domain) and writes `tools/roads.js`, pasted into the page. It has 456 nodes and 20 cities, and Madrid to Barcelona comes out at 589 km. Weather is from Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0), cached for half an hour, and the toy works without it.

## Rough or left out
The road's curves and hills are generated rather than taken from the real polyline's bends, and the side view has no passing traffic. There are no junctions or exits yet, so the indicators only change lanes. Road names are mostly bare E-route numbers from the data. There is only one car, so there's no garage, and the cockpit doesn't change at the stop. The towers on the sides of the road are big and a little blocky up close. Signs with real city names appear about every 2 km. They are drawn with the system sans-serif rather than a pixel font.

## Next three things
1. Use the real heading changes of the route's polyline for the curves, and add exits, so an indicator can ask for a turn at a junction.
2. More life on the road and at the stops: oncoming headlights, jams and roadworks, a beach and a field for the stop scene, and the cat as a pet that stays after a delivery.
3. A garage of two or three cars, each with its own cockpit sheet, unlocked by deliveries.
